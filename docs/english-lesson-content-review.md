# Beginner lesson content review

Reviewed 18 September 2026. Source: `public/templates/english-lessons.xlsx`, all 14 lesson sheets and Guide. This is an editorial review and proposed replacement content, not an applied workbook update. Browser-saved lessons may differ from the template, especially after quick edits. No browser library was overwritten.

## Overall finding

The pack has useful everyday topics, Vietnamese support, worked examples, and opportunities to ask for help. Its main weakness is that activity quantity has become a substitute for evidence of learning. It needs clearer teaching and more purposeful practice, not simply more content.

There are 565 activities: 304 fill-in-the-blank, 101 listen-and-repeat, 84 read-and-answer, 23 word-order, 22 dictation, 18 correction, and 13 read-aloud. Of these, 201 prompts explicitly give a first-letter vocabulary clue. These counts describe task formats, not measured learning effectiveness.

Ten lessons, from Foundation 1 through Day 7, use identical teacher notes. Extra practice usually repeats the first three vocabulary examples rather than addressing the lesson's most difficult skill. The app's seven exercise formats need not all appear in every lesson: the format should serve the objective.

## Changes that matter most

1. Give each lesson a small, observable outcome. For example, “Hear a classroom instruction and do the correct action” is more useful than “understand instructions.” Let students recognise their achievement through something they can do.
2. Teach before testing. For every important pattern, provide its purpose, a complete example, a small change to the example, and a short task. Vietnamese explanations should remain available after the Start lessons, too.
3. Replace vague quick checks with actual questions and an answer key. A first-ever lesson can have a supported warm-up rather than a prerequisite test. Do not make students pass material the lesson has not yet taught.
4. Keep recall practice, but move much of the repetitive spelling work to Extra. Use Core for understanding, useful sentence patterns, listening, and choosing an appropriate response. Retain all vocabulary as a reference if useful; students need not master every entry in one sitting.
5. Use short, meaningful exchanges. Repeating both sides of a dialogue is pronunciation rehearsal; it does not demonstrate that the learner can choose a reply. Include role changes and prompts with new names, places, or preferences.
6. Give explanations that teach. After an incorrect answer, identify the relevant clue or pattern, then offer a new example. Repeating the target word's example sentence is often insufficient.
7. Make the scoring contract clear. For machine-scored questions, request a specific answer form or list valid alternatives. Put personal, creative answers in the unscored challenge. A word-match percentage must not be presented as mastery of communication.
8. Keep teacher instructions out of learner notes. Demonstration instructions, pacing, and what to observe belong in Teacher notes. Learner sections should explain what to do now.

Spelling appears in an understanding check although it is described as optional preparation. | Prioritise a greeting, name exchange, polite response, and goodbye. | Day 4 Likes | The grammar section includes like/love/enjoy, noun and -ing complements, infinitive alternatives, third-person -s, favourites, and prefer A to B. | Day 5 Questions | Much vocabulary repeats S2. Abstract words such as reaction and information distract from the final conversation outcome. | Build review around a short introduction, listening for another person's details, two questions, and a follow-up. ## Suggested Start 1 replacement text

These are proposed learner-facing notes. Keep the existing import field names. Do not add bracketed stage directions to speech text: the current audio player may read them aloud.

**Goal**

Follow five simple instructions: listen, repeat, read, write, and stop. / Làm theo năm yêu cầu đơn giản: nghe, nhắc lại, đọc, viết và dừng lại.

**Before you start**

Bạn chưa cần biết tiếng Anh. Chuẩn bị giấy và bút, hoặc dùng ô trả lời trong bài. Hôm nay chỉ cần làm theo năm yêu cầu: Listen, Repeat, Read, Write và Stop. Nếu chưa đọc được phần hướng dẫn, hãy nhờ người thân đọc cùng.

**Quick check — supported warm-up**

Xem mẫu: Listen = nghe; Write = viết.
1. Khi nghe “Listen”, bạn nghe hay viết?
2. Khi thấy “Write hello”, bạn viết từ nào?
Tự đối chiếu sau khi thử: 1. Nghe. 2. hello.
Chưa nhớ cũng không sao; phần dưới sẽ hướng dẫn từng bước.

**Grammar & sentence patterns**

Listen. = Hãy nghe. Bạn nghe câu mẫu; chưa cần viết.
Repeat. = Hãy nhắc lại. Nghe “Hello”, rồi nói “Hello”.
Read. = Hãy đọc. Nhìn chữ hello và đọc để hiểu. “Read aloud” yêu cầu đọc thành tiếng.
Write hello. = Hãy viết từ hello, không viết cả lời hướng dẫn.
Stop. = Hãy dừng việc đang làm.
Listen and repeat. = Nghe trước, rồi nói lại cùng lời mẫu.
Thêm please để yêu cầu lịch sự: Listen, please. / Please listen.

**Common phrases**

Listen, please. → Hãy nghe.
Listen and repeat. → Nghe rồi nhắc lại.
Read the word. → Đọc từ đang hiện ra.
Write the word. → Viết từ được yêu cầu.
Please say it again. → Xin nói lại. Dùng khi bạn chưa nghe rõ.

**Pronunciation tips**

Nghe “listen”, rồi nhắc lại. Chữ t trong từ này không được đọc.
Nghe “repeat”, rồi nhắc lại; phần “peat” được nhấn mạnh.
Nói “Listen, please” thành một cụm ngắn. Nghe lại và thử thêm một lần nếu cần.

**Pronunciation model (English)**

Listen. Repeat. Read. Write. Stop. Listen, please. Listen and repeat. Please say it again.

**Common mistakes**

Listen = nghe. Write = viết. “Listen and write” yêu cầu nghe trước, rồi viết nội dung nghe được.
Repeat yêu cầu nói lại lời mẫu. Nghe “Hello” thì nói “Hello”, không nói từ “repeat”.
“Write hello” yêu cầu viết hello. “Write the missing word” chỉ yêu cầu phần còn thiếu: Say ___. (hello) → hello.

**Model conversation**

A: Say hello, please.
B: Hello.
A: Say goodbye, please.
B: Please say it again.
A: Say goodbye.
B: Goodbye.
A: Thank you.

Introduce hello = xin chào and goodbye = tạm biệt beside the dialogue before playing it. Keep this explanation outside the A/B audio text.

**Personal challenge**

Nhờ người cùng học chọn ba yêu cầu theo thứ tự bất kỳ: Listen, Repeat, Read, Write hoặc Stop. Họ dùng hello hoặc goodbye làm từ mẫu. Bạn làm theo, rồi đổi vai. Nếu học một mình, nghe một câu mẫu, nói lại, rồi viết từ đó và đối chiếu. Bạn đã hoàn thành khi làm đúng ba yêu cầu; có thể xin nhắc lại.

**Teacher notes**

Demonstrate listening without writing, repeating aloud, reading a visible word, and writing it. Ask “Nghe hay viết?” to check the action. Begin with five instructions; introduce the remaining instruction words only when their exercise appears. Notice whether an error comes from the instruction, the English word, or typing. Praise a specific success: “You listened first and then wrote hello.” If the learner is stuck, demonstrate one item together and give a different item to try. Do not treat a correct copied word as proof of independent understanding.

## Proposed practice progression

For Start 1, use a small sequence of models and choices before spelling recall: hear and repeat hello; distinguish listen/write; choose the action for Read aloud; follow Write hello; complete Say ___ with a supplied clue; use a new instruction independently. Place the full instruction-vocabulary spelling list in Extra. Include brief feedback explaining the action.

For later lessons, use the same learning progression without duplicating the same exercises: see/hear a meaningful model → notice one pattern → practise with support → change one detail → use it in a short exchange → return later with a fresh example. This is an editorial recommendation, not a fixed number of tasks or a guarantee of mastery.

## Implementation boundaries and validation

- Current spreadsheet fields support all proposed note rewrites and Core/Extra rebalancing. They do not provide interactive picture matching, hidden warm-up answer keys, or listening multiple choice. Those require product work; do not label a read-and-answer task as a listening test.
- A note's answer key is ordinary visible text. Until there is a reveal control, put it after the questions and explicitly ask learners to try first.
- Preserve lesson titles during a first revision so imports can suggest matching existing lessons. Verify update selection rather than adding duplicate lessons.
- Review every changed prompt together with Content, Answer, and Explanation. Check pronoun perspective, optional contractions, spelling variants, and whether a blank permits more than one valid completion.
- Preview spoken text for letters, numbers, dates, and speaker turns. A correct written transcript alone does not validate synthesized audio.
- Run the actual workbook importer and content checks after editing. Existing tests asserting large Core counts or duplicated exercises should be replaced with meaningful coverage checks if the curriculum is rebalanced.
- This review has not tested learning outcomes with students, listened to every synthesized model, or independently verified every IPA. Those are separate checks. Existing browser imports will not change merely because the downloadable template changes.

## Teaching references

The British Council recommends staged instructions, simple language, demonstrations, and short questions to check that learners know what to do: [Communicate clearly](https://africa.teachingenglish.org.uk/skills/educate-other-teachers/communicate-information-instructions).

Its guidance also recommends checking understanding of vocabulary, instructions, and texts through questions that reveal understanding: [Checking understanding](https://africa.teachingenglish.org.uk/skills/tips/checking-understanding).

These principles inform the recommendations above; they do not certify this pack or prescribe the proposed activity distribution.
